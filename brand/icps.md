# Ideal customer profiles

These are the five people Harborline writes to. Use them with `brand/audience.md` and `brand/voice.md`. The colors and layout stay in `brand/design-system.md`.

A profile is not a persona sketch for a slide. It is the brief for the letter.

---

## 1. First-time visitor

**Use this ICP for a welcome email, a first booking, or any letter to someone who has not sailed with Harborline.**

Mara is visiting for four days. She booked the 06:15 from Kelsall Quay to Nethergill aboard Ardmore Rose. She has never stood on this quay. She does not know when the desk closes, what happens if the wind gets up, or that we will still help her if she has not booked assistance. She is not on holiday from the idea of ferries. She needs to get to Nethergill in the morning.

### What she already knows

Almost nothing about us. She has a booking confirmation from the form and a time.

### What the letter must do

- Confirm the booking in the first sentence.
- Name the crossing, the vessel, the terminal, the time, and the date.
- Teach the day: foot passengers 20 minutes early, vehicles 30 minutes, what happens if she is late.
- Say that help is available without a speech about values.
- Say that we cancel when it is not safe, and that a canceled scheduled sailing is refunded. No weather exclusion.

### What the letter must never do

- Welcome her to a family, a community, or a journey.
- Describe the view, the islands, or the "experience".
- Sell a return, a day ticket, or Little Ossian.
- Assume she knows Kelsall Quay from Nethergill.

### How the voice lands

Same Harborline voice, a little more teaching. Short sentences. Named places. No warmth that has to be announced.

Subject: `Your first sailing with Harborline`

Opening:

> Your booking is confirmed. We will see you on the quay.

Close: the full on-the-day block from `emails/welcome.md`.

Wrong:

> We are so excited to welcome you aboard for an unforgettable coastal escape.

## 2. Resident commuter

Calum lives in Nethergill and works near Kelsall Quay. He knows Ardmore Rose from the window. He does not need to be told what a terminal is, and he will delete a letter that explains Harborline to him.

### What he already knows

The crossings, the vessels, the check-in habit, the weather. He is reading the letter for the time, the vessel, and whether anything moved.

### What the letter must do

- Confirm the sailing and the time in the first sentence.
- Name the vessel only so he can see if it changed.
- Mention a change if there is one: a substitute vessel, a diverted berth, a night restriction at Kelsall Quay.
- Stop. Do not teach the day.

### What the letter must never do

- Use the subject `Your first sailing with Harborline`.
- Explain check-in, assistance, or who we are.
- Wish him a pleasant crossing.
- Write as if he is visiting.

### How the voice lands

Shorter than the welcome letter. Still Georgia, still Hull Navy, still `See you on the quay`. No primer.

Subject: `Kelsall Quay 16:20, March 4`

Opening:

> You are on the 16:20 from Nethergill to Kelsall Quay aboard Kelsall Endeavour.

If nothing else changed, that can be the whole body before the button.

Wrong:

> Just a quick reminder of everything you need to know before you travel with us!

## 3. Vehicle traveler

Priya is bringing a van on the outer crossing. The fact that matters is the lane, not the passenger lounge. If the wind goes into the restricted-deck band, high-sided vehicles stay ashore. She needs that in plain words before she leaves for the quay.

### What she already knows

That she booked a vehicle space. Not always which class she is on, or that Class A and Class B close the lane at different times.

### What the letter must do

- Confirm the sailing and that a vehicle is on the booking.
- Put vehicle check-in first: join the lane 30 minutes before. Class A closes 25 minutes before, Class B 15 minutes before.
- Say what happens if she misses the lane: she is not on that sailing, and we put her on the next one at no charge.
- If the vessel is Class A or B, say so. Little Ossian takes no vehicles. If a vehicle booking has been placed on Little Ossian, the booking is wrong and must be moved. Tell her.
- In winter or on an exposed crossing, one sentence on the vehicle deck: in strong wind we may take foot passengers only, and high-sided vehicles wait.

### What the letter must never do

- Lead with foot-passenger times and tuck the van in a footnote.
- Say "cars welcome" or describe the deck.
- Promise the van will sail in any weather.

### How the voice lands

Operational. The van is the subject, not a lifestyle detail.

Subject: `Your vehicle is booked on the 09:30 to Nethergill`

Opening:

> Your van is booked on the 09:30 from Kelsall Quay to Nethergill aboard Kelsall Endeavour. Join the vehicle lane 30 minutes before departure.

Wrong:

> Pack the snacks and hit the road. Your adventure starts at the ramp!

## 4. Charter booker

The Harrisons hired Little Ossian for a circular from Kelsall Quay. They are a **customer**, not a passenger, in the letter. The scheduled-service refund table does not apply. Little Ossian has no vehicle deck and no lift.

### What they already knows

The date, the fee, and that they hired a boat. Not always that weather cancels a charter above 28 knots with a fare refund only, or that a wheelchair booking cannot stay on Little Ossian.

### What the letter must do

- Confirm the charter: date, time, vessel, pick-up terminal, and that it is a private hire.
- Give Little Ossian check-in: foot passengers 20 minutes before. No vehicles.
- If anyone in the party needs wheelchair access, say the booking must move to a Class B vessel, and that we will tell them and arrange it. Do not leave it for the ramp.
- State the charter cancel terms only if the letter is about a change or they asked. A welcome letter can point once: weather is a full refund of the fee and nothing more. A customer cancel inside 7 days is no refund.
- Do not pay the £30 per-passenger scheduled compensation on a charter. Do not imply it.

### What the letter must never do

- Call it a sailing in the same breath as the 06:15 commuter boat without saying it is a charter.
- Apply the delay table from `brand/customer-facts.md` section 7.1.
- Write "welcome aboard the Harborline fleet".
- Promise Little Ossian will run in any wind.

### How the voice lands

Still plain. A little more contractual, never stiff. They hired a vessel. Treat that as a fact.

Subject: `Your charter on Little Ossian, March 4`

Opening:

> Your charter is confirmed. Little Ossian leaves Kelsall Quay at 12:00 on March 4. This is a private hire, not a scheduled crossing.

Wrong:

> Congratulations on booking your exclusive Harborline experience for the whole family!

## 5. Assisted traveler

Jonah booked assistance for the estuary crossing on Tolquin Spirit. He may be traveling himself, or booking for someone he is with. He has heard "book 24 hours ahead" and is waiting to hear whether that is a condition of carriage. It is not.

### What he already knows

That he asked for a hand. He does not know our rule until we say it.

### What the letter must do

- Confirm the sailing, then confirm the arrangement.
- Say assisted travel can be booked up to 24 hours before departure. A passenger who arrives without having booked is still carried and still helped.
- Tell him where to ask: any check-in desk, or any crew member, for a discreet request card. No explanation is asked for.
- Name the constraint that affects him. Little Ossian has no lift. A wheelchair booking on Little Ossian is moved to a Class B vessel. We tell the passenger. If that is this booking, say what we changed and when.
- Assistance dogs are carried on every vessel, at no charge. Mention it only if one is on the booking.

### What the letter must never do

- Make help sound like a perk, a program, or a brand value.
- Ask what the disability is, or write it back to him.
- Say he must have booked or he will not be carried.
- Use the words brave, special, or inspiring.

### How the voice lands

The same desk voice as everyone else. The extra sentences are facts about the arrangement, not a softer tone.

Subject: `Your sailing on Tolquin Spirit, and the arrangement we made`

Opening:

> You are booked on the 07:00 from Tolquin Pier to Braewick Slip aboard Tolquin Spirit. Assistance is arranged. If you arrive without having booked it, we still carry you and we still help.

Wrong:

> We are honored to support you on your journey and are here for you every step of the way.
