# Harborline voice

Harborline speaks the way the quay works: plainly, specifically, and without fuss. A passenger should finish a letter knowing what is happening, what they need to do, and that nobody is selling them a feeling.

## Who we are talking to

People who live here, people who are visiting, and people who booked a charter. They need a sailing, a time, and a berth. They do not need a brand story.

Write as Harborline Ferries, from Kelsall Quay. Do not write as a marketing team, a chatbot, or "the Harborline family".

## Tone

Warm enough to be human. Spare enough to be trusted.

- Direct. Say the thing in the first sentence.
- Local. Name the terminal, the vessel, and the crossing.
- Calm. Weather and delay are ordinary facts, not drama.
- Kind without performance. Help is offered. It is never advertised as a value.

If a sentence would sound odd said across a check-in desk, cut it.

## Words we use

- Harborline, never Harbourline, never Harbor Line.
- Sailing, crossing, quay, berth, terminal, vessel.
- Passenger, not customer, except for charter bookings.
- Fare, not ticket price. Booking, not order.
- Cancelled, delayed, on time. Those three, not "impacted" or "experiencing disruption".
- See you on the quay. That is the only sign-off.

Vessel names are written in full: Ardmore Rose, Kelsall Endeavour, Marnoch Star, Tolquin Spirit, Braewick Trader, Little Ossian. Never "the Rose" or "the Ossian".

Terminals are Kelsall Quay, Nethergill, Tolquin Pier, and Braewick Slip. Never "our terminal" when the name is known.

## Words we do not use

Never:

- delighted, thrilled, excited, passionate
- journey (unless a passenger is literally travelling)
- family, community, family of brands
- seamless, hassle-free, world-class, iconic
- "we're here for you", "we've got you", "sit back and relax"
- exclamation marks
- emoji
- questions we already know the answer to ("Ready for your adventure?")

Do not write "sorry for the inconvenience" on a weather cancellation. Write what happened, what we will do, and what they are entitled to.

## How a letter is built

1. **Open with the fact.** "Your booking is confirmed." "The 16:20 from Kelsall Quay is cancelled." Not a preamble.
2. **Give the particulars.** Route, vessel, terminal, time, date. One line each or a short list.
3. **Tell them what to do on the day.** Check-in times, what happens if they are late, how to ask for help.
4. **Name the uncomfortable thing if there is one.** Weather, a delay, a moved booking. Do it in the same register as the rest.
5. **Close.** `See you on the quay` then `Harborline Ferries`.

One letter, one purpose. A welcome letter does not also sell a day-return. A cancellation letter does not also welcome them aboard.

## Rhythm and grammar

- British English. Colour, harbour (in ordinary prose about harbours), organise, cancelled. The company name stays Harborline.
- Short sentences. A full stop is better than a semicolon.
- No em dash. A comma or a new sentence.
- Percent is written `per cent`.
- Times are 24-hour: 06:15, 16:20. Dates are `4 March`, not `March 4th`.
- We, not I. Harborline, not "our team".
- Do not hedge. "The sailing is cancelled" not "the sailing may unfortunately need to be cancelled".

## Honesty rules

These are company rules, not style tips.

- If we cancel a scheduled sailing, the passenger gets their fare back. There is no weather exclusion. Do not invent one.
- A late passenger who misses check-in is put on the next sailing at no charge. Say so.
- Assisted travel can be booked up to 24 hours before departure. A passenger who arrives without having booked is still carried and still helped. Never imply that notice is a condition of carriage.
- A delayed passenger is offered the claim form. They are never told to ask later.
- Little Ossian has no lift and no vehicle deck. A wheelchair booking is moved to a Class B vessel. Tell the passenger. Do not leave them to discover it at the ramp.

## Sample lines

Use these as the register. Do not paste them into the wrong letter.

Welcome:

> Your booking is confirmed. We will see you on the quay.

Check-in:

> Foot passengers should be at the desk 20 minutes before departure. Check-in closes 15 minutes before on the outer crossings and 10 minutes before on the estuary boats.

Late arrival:

> If you arrive after check-in has closed, we will put you on the next sailing at no charge.

Assistance:

> If you need a hand, ask at any check-in desk. You do not have to have booked, and you do not have to explain.

Weather:

> We sail when it is safe and we do not sail when it is not. If we cancel, you get your fare back. We will write as soon as the decision is made.

Cancellation:

> The 16:20 from Kelsall Quay to Nethergill on 4 March is cancelled. Your fare will be returned to the card you paid with within 14 days.

Wrong register, do not write:

> We're so excited to welcome you to the Harborline family! Get ready for an unforgettable journey across the water.

## Length

A welcome email is about 180 to 220 words. A cancellation is shorter. If a letter needs more than one screen, something else is being smuggled in. Take it out.
